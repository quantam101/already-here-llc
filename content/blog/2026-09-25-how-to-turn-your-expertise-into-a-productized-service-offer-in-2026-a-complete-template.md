## From Open‑Ended Hours to Fixed Outcomes: What You’ll Build Today  

You’ve spent years mastering a skill—whether it’s copywriting, data visualization, or WordPress maintenance—and you’re tired of the “how many hours?” question that drags your rates down. By the end of this guide you will have a **productized‑service offer template** that includes:  

* A clear scope of work (what’s in and out)  
* A fixed price and turnaround promise  
* Proof elements you can show prospects instantly  
* An intake questionnaire that filters the right clients  
* A repeatable delivery checklist that lets you ship the service on autopilot  

With that template in hand you can launch a side hustle that sells outcomes, not time, and start making money online with predictable cash flow.

---

## Step 1: Choose the Core Outcome You’ll Deliver  

A productized service is defined by the **result**, not the activity. Start by answering three questions:  

1. **What is the single, measurable outcome my client cares about?**  
2. **How can I guarantee that outcome within a realistic timeframe?**  
3. **Is the outcome repeatable for multiple clients without custom engineering each time?**  

*Example*: If you’re a graphic designer who usually does “design work,” reframe it as **“Brand‑Ready Visual Identity Kit in 7 days.”** The outcome is a complete kit (logo, color palette, typography guide) delivered on a set schedule.

### Decision criteria checklist  

| Criterion | Yes/No | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Outcome is **tangible** (file, report, website) |  |  |
| Outcome can be **standardized** (same steps for every client) |  |  |
| You can **measure** success (e.g., 5 deliverables, 2 revisions) |  |  |
| Market demand exists (searches, forum requests) |  |  |

If any row is “No,” revisit the skill until you find a version that meets all four criteria.

---

## Step 2: Define the Service Scope (In‑Scope vs. Out‑of‑Scope)  

A crisp scope protects you from scope creep and tells prospects exactly what they’re buying. Use a two‑column table:

| In‑Scope (What you’ll do) | Out‑of‑Scope (What you won’t do) |
|---------------------------|----------------------------------|
| 5 custom logo concepts | Full brand strategy workshops |
| 2 rounds of revisions | Unlimited revisions |
| Delivery of assets in AI, PNG, SVG | Development of marketing copy |

**Why it matters:** When a client asks for something outside the box, you can politely refer them to an “add‑on” or a separate project, keeping the core offer lean.

---

## Step 3: Set a Fixed Price and Turnaround Time  

### 3.1 Calculate a baseline cost  

1. **Hourly baseline** – Determine the hourly rate you would normally charge (e.g., $80/hr).  
2. **Estimated hours** – Count the hours needed to complete the defined scope (e.g., 12 hours).  
3. **Base price** = Hourly rate × Estimated hours → $960.  

### 3.2 Add a margin for risk and profit  

*Risk buffer* (10‑15%) covers unexpected revisions.  
*Profit margin* (20‑30%) reflects the value of selling an outcome.  

**Formula:**  
`Final price = Base price × (1 + Risk % + Profit %)`  

### 3.3 Example (hypothetical)  

| Component | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Hourly rate | $80 |
| Estimated hours | 12 |
| Base price | $960 |
| Risk buffer (15%) | $144 |
| Profit margin (25%) | $240 |
| **Fixed price** | **$1,344** |

Round to a clean number that feels market‑appropriate (e.g., **$1,350**).  

### 3.4 Turnaround  

Pick a timeframe that balances client expectations with your capacity. For the visual identity kit, **7 business days** is realistic and creates urgency.

## Step 4: Build Proof Elements You Can Show Instantly  

Prospects need confidence that you can deliver. Assemble three proof assets:  

1. **Portfolio piece** – A recent project that matches the new scope.  
2. **Case study snapshot** – One‑paragraph result (e.g., “Client X launched with a new visual identity and saw a 12% increase in website sign‑ups in the first month”).  
3. **Client testimonial** – A short quote that mentions speed and outcome.  

Host these on a simple landing page or a PDF one‑pager you can attach to outreach emails.

---

## Step 5: Design an Intake Questionnaire  

A short, structured form filters qualified leads and gathers everything you need to start. Keep it under 7 fields to avoid friction. Example fields for the visual identity kit:  

1. **Company name & website**  
2. **Primary business goal for the new brand**  
3. **Preferred style adjectives (e.g., modern, playful)**  
4. **Existing brand assets (if any)** – upload file  
5. **Target launch date**  
6. **Budget confirmation** – “My budget aligns with the $1,350 price.”  
7. **Contact preference** – email or calendar link  

Use a free form tool (Google Forms, Typeform) and embed the link in your sales page.

---

## Step 6: Create a Repeatable Delivery Checklist  

A checklist guarantees you don’t miss a step and lets you hand off the process to a future team member. Break the workflow into phases:

### Phase 1 – Discovery (Day 1)  
- ☐ Review intake responses  
- ☐ Schedule 15‑minute kickoff call (optional)  
- ☐ Confirm payment receipt  

### Phase 2 – Concept Development (Day 2‑4)  
- ☐ Draft 5 logo concepts  
- ☐ Create mood board based on style adjectives  
- ☐ Prepare initial color palette  

### Phase 3 – Review & Revision (Day 5‑6)  
- ☐ Send concepts to client with brief feedback form  
- ☐ Incorporate up to 2 rounds of revisions  

### Phase 4 – Final Delivery (Day 7)  
- ☐ Export assets in AI, PNG, SVG  
- ☐ Compile brand guide PDF (logo usage, colors, fonts)  
- ☐ Upload files to client‑shared folder and email delivery note  

Store this checklist in a project management tool (Notion, Trello) and duplicate it for each new client.

## Step 7: Draft the Offer Page Using the Template  

Below is a ready‑to‑copy skeleton you can paste into a website builder or a PDF one‑pager.

```
**[Service Name] – Brand‑Ready Visual Identity Kit**  
*Your brand, ready to launch in 7 days for $1,350.*

**What’s Included**  
- 5 custom logo concepts  
- 2 rounds of revisions  
- Full color palette & typography guide  
- Deliverables in AI, PNG, SVG  

**What’s Not Included**  
- Full brand strategy workshops  
- Unlimited revisions  
- Marketing copywriting  

**How It Works**  
1. Fill out the 5‑minute intake form.  
2. Pay the fixed fee via Stripe.  
3. Receive concepts on Day 2, final assets on Day 7.  

**Proof**  
[Insert portfolio thumbnail]  
“[Client testimonial]”

**Ready to start?**  
[Link to intake form] → *Your brand is 7 days away.*
```

Replace brackets with your own branding and links.

## Step 8: Test the Offer Before Going Live  

1. **Run a pilot with a friend or a low‑budget client** – treat it as a real sale, collect feedback on the intake form, turnaround, and checklist.  
2. **Adjust scope or price** if the pilot reveals hidden work or pricing gaps.  
3. **Document the changes** in your template so future offers stay consistent.

---

## Step 9: Launch and Automate the Front‑End  

While the core service remains manual, the front‑end can be automated:  

* **Landing page** – host the offer page on a simple site (Carrd, Webflow).  
* **Payment** – embed a Stripe “Buy Now” button that triggers an email receipt.  
* **Intake** – set the Stripe receipt to include the intake form link.  

If you prefer a ready‑made solution, consider a **contract template** that includes scope, price, and timeline. A popular option is the **[HelloSign Contract Template](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=HelloSign+contract+template&tag=alreadyhere-20&linkCode=ll2)**, which can be customized and signed electronically.

---

## Step 10: Scale with Add‑Ons and Retainers  

Once the core productized service runs smoothly, you can increase revenue by offering:  

* **Add‑on services** (e.g., brand guidelines PDF, social media templates) priced per unit.  
* **Monthly retainers** for ongoing design tweaks or brand updates.  

Keep each add‑on as a separate, fixed‑price package to preserve the “outcome” mindset.
