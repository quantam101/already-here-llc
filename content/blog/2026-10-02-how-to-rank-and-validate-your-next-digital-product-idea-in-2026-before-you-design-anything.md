## You Have Too Many Ideas and Not Enough Hours – Here’s What You’ll Achieve  
If you’re juggling concepts for SVG files, printable planners, and other Etsy digital downloads, the biggest obstacle is deciding which one deserves your limited design time. By the end of this guide you will:  

1. Apply a six‑factor scoring matrix to any digital product idea.  
2. Produce a ranked shortlist of your top three concepts.  
3. Set up a cheap, data‑driven validation test that tells you whether the highest‑scoring idea is worth building.  

Everything is laid out as a repeatable decision framework, so you can reuse it for every new wave of inspiration.  

## Step 1 – Capture Every Idea in a Single Sheet  
Before you can compare, you need a neutral list. Open a spreadsheet (Google Sheets, Airtable, or a simple CSV file works). Create columns for:  

| Idea | Category | Brief Description | Date Captured |  
|------|----------|-------------------|---------------|  

Enter each raw concept exactly as it came to you – no judgment, no details yet. For example:  

* “Floral SVG clip‑set for wedding invitations”  
* “2026 Minimalist printable daily planner”  
* “Etsy bundle of 10 seasonal digital wall art files”  

Having a master list prevents ideas from slipping away while you evaluate them.  

## Step 2 – Score Each Idea Against Six Validation Factors  
The core of the framework is a 1‑to‑5 rating (1 = very weak, 5 = very strong) for each factor. Add a “Total Score” column that sums the six ratings. Below is a description of each factor and how to gather the data.  

### 1. Demand Signals  
*What to look for:* Search volume on Etsy, Google Trends spikes, Pinterest pin counts, Reddit mentions.  
*How to collect:*  
- Use Etsy’s search bar autocomplete to see how many suggestions appear for key terms.  
- Open Google Trends (set the region to your primary market) and type the core keyword; note the “interest over time” line.  
- On Pinterest, type the keyword and glance at the “pins” count shown at the top.  

Assign a score: 5 if you see consistent upward trends across at least two platforms, 3 for moderate interest, 1 for negligible signals.  

### 2. Customer Language Alignment  
*What to look for:* The exact words buyers use in reviews, forum posts, and social media comments.  
*How to collect:*  
- Search Etsy for similar products and copy five recent buyer reviews.  
- Scan relevant subreddits (e.g., r/Etsy, r/Design) for the phrase “I wish there was…”.  
- Highlight recurring adjectives (e.g., “editable”, “high‑resolution”, “instant download”).  

Score 5 when your idea’s description can be phrased using the top three buyer‑spoken terms without awkward rewording. Score lower if you need to stretch the language.  

### 3. Competing Offer Saturation  
*What to look for:* Number of existing listings, price clustering, and average review count.  
*How to collect:*  
- Search Etsy with the core keyword and note the total results (Etsy shows this at the top).  
- Filter by “Best selling” and count how many items appear on the first two pages.  
- Record the median price of those listings.  

Score 5 for niches with fewer than 50 total listings and low average review counts (indicating room to stand out). Score 1 for heavily saturated categories with hundreds of listings and tight price competition.  

### 4. Differentiation Potential  
*What to look for:* A clear hook that competitors don’t already claim.  
*How to collect:*  
- List the top three differentiators you could offer (e.g., “hand‑drawn botanical style”, “editable layers for Canva”, “bundled with a tutorial video”).  
- Verify none of the top 10 competitors mention the same hook.  

Score 5 if you can name at least two unique angles; score 1 if the idea is a carbon copy of existing products.  

### 5. Production Effort  
*What to look for:* Estimated hours to create a market‑ready version, plus any software or hardware costs.  
*How to collect:*  
- Break the product into components (e.g., 20 SVG files, 5 color palettes).  
- Estimate hours per component based on past projects.  
- Add a buffer of 20 % for revisions.  

Score 5 for ≤ 8 hours total, 3 for 9‑20 hours, 1 for > 20 hours.  

### 6. Support Burden  
*What to look for:* Expected post‑sale questions (file compatibility, licensing, updates).  
*How to collect:*  
- Review the FAQ sections of similar Etsy listings.  
- Count distinct support topics (e.g., “How do I open the file in Illustrator?”).  

Score 5 when support topics are ≤ 2 and can be answered with a static FAQ; score 1 when you anticipate ongoing custom requests or troubleshooting.  

### Example Scoring Sheet (Hypothetical)  

| Idea | Demand | Language | Competition | Differentiation | Effort | Support | Total |
|------|--------|----------|-------------|----------------|--------|---------|-------|
| Floral SVG Clip‑set | 4 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 5 | **26** |
| Minimalist Daily Planner | 3 | 4 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 4 | **20** |
| Seasonal Wall Art Bundle | 2 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 3 | 3 | **17** |

*Assumptions*: Scores are based on publicly observable data (search results, trend graphs) and the creator’s own time estimates.  

The highest total (26) becomes your **primary candidate** for validation.  

## Step 3 – Design a Low‑Cost Validation Test  
Now that you have a clear front‑runner, you need proof that real buyers will pay for it—without spending weeks on design. Follow this three‑day “Mini‑MVP” process.  

### Day 1 – Build a Rough Prototype  
- Create **one** representative asset (e.g., a single floral SVG file) using a free vector editor like Inkscape or a trial of Affinity Designer.  
- Export it in the formats buyers expect (SVG, PNG, EPS).  
- Write a concise product description that mirrors the customer language you gathered earlier.  

### Day 2 – Set Up a Test Listing on Etsy (or a simple landing page)  
- Open an Etsy shop if you don’t already have one; the listing fee is a fixed amount per item, so the upfront cost is low.  
- Upload the prototype file, set the price at the median you observed in Step 2 (e.g., $5).  
- Use the “Preview” mode to generate a shareable link without publishing the item publicly.  

### Day 3 – Drive Targeted, Cheap Traffic  
- Post the link in two relevant places where your ideal buyer hangs out: a niche Facebook group and a subreddit.  
- Offer a **“first‑buyer discount”** (e.g., 20 % off) to encourage clicks.  
- Track two metrics:  
  1. Click‑through rate (CTR) on the link.  
  2. Conversion rate (purchases ÷ clicks).  

**Decision rule:** If CTR ≥ 5 % **and** conversion ≥ 2 %, you have a viable market signal. If either metric falls short, consider iterating the hook or moving to the next‑ranked idea.  

### Why This Test Works  
- **Cost:** Etsy listing fee + minimal ad spend (optional) stays under $20.  
- **Speed:** You only need one asset, not a full bundle.  
- **Data‑driven:** Real purchase intent is more reliable than “likes” or “shares.”  

## Step 4 – Interpret Results and Choose Your Path  
| Outcome | Action |
|---------|--------|
| **Both metrics meet the rule** | Proceed to full production. Use the prototype’s feedback to refine the final bundle. |
| **Only CTR meets the rule** | Your hook resonates but pricing or perceived value may be off. Run a quick price‑sensitivity test (e.g., $3 vs $7). |
| **Neither metric meets the rule** | Archive the idea or revisit the scoring sheet to see which factor dragged it down. Consider a different angle or a new idea from your list. |

## Trade‑offs, Risks, and Cost Considerations  
- **Time vs Depth:** The six‑factor matrix is quick, but deeper research (e.g., paid keyword tools) can improve accuracy. Decide whether the extra cost fits your budget.  
- **Platform Dependency:** Etsy’s search algorithm changes; always verify that the demand signals you captured are still visible before launching.  
- **False Positives:** A small, highly engaged community may inflate CTR. Counter this by testing in at least two distinct traffic sources.  
- **Support Overhead:** Even a simple SVG file can generate licensing questions. Draft a clear license statement early to keep support time low.
